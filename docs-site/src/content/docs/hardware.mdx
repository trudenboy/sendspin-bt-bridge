title: Hardware
description: Controllers, paired Bluetooth devices, and the computer's audio devices with their inputs and outputs
---

The **Hardware** page shows the physical layer the bridge drives, whether or not a
player uses it: Bluetooth controllers, the devices paired to them, and the audio
devices of the host with their outputs and inputs.

It is deliberately separate from the speakers dashboard. A speaker is a player; a
controller, a paired phone or a sound card is hardware. A device that no player
points at — a phone that is not streaming, a second adapter with nothing on it —
is still listed here.

## Controllers

One card per Bluetooth adapter:

- power state, with the control to turn it off or on. Turning an adapter off
  disconnects every speaker that uses it, and the confirmation names them;
- the friendly name you gave it in the configuration;
- the class of device the kernel reports (empty when the container has no
  `CAP_NET_RAW`);
- the number of paired devices that use this adapter;
- the Home Assistant area, when area mapping is configured.

A device can be bonded to more than one adapter. It then appears under each of
them, and its own card lists every adapter that holds the bond.

## Bluetooth devices

Paired and known devices, with what the bridge knows about each:

- connected, paired, bonded, trusted;
- battery level, when the device reports it over `org.bluez.Battery1`;
- signal strength, when the kernel reports it — a delta inside the BR/EDR golden
  receive range, not dBm;
- the **role** of the link: **Plays audio** when the bridge sends sound to the
  device, **Receives audio** when the device sends sound to the bridge. A device
  can show both at once.
- the player it backs, with a link to that player.

Actions: **Connect**, **Disconnect**, **Add as speaker** (for a device that is
not yet a player), **Pair a device** (the same scanning flow the speakers
dashboard uses), and **Forget bond**.

Forgetting a bond that a player depends on is refused: the card says which player
it is and links to it, because removing the player is the action that makes
sense. The bridge never silently leaves a player without a device.

## Audio

The audio server's own view of the host:

- **Cards** with their profiles and ports. When an output is silent because its
  active port is not available or its profile is `off`, this is where you see it.
  Port availability is three-valued: available, not available (unplugged), and
  unknown.
- **Outputs** with state, volume, mute, the player that uses each one, and a
  **Default** badge on the host's default output. Volume, mute and the default
  output can be set from here, and an output can be paused to free it.
- **Inputs** with their kind: a microphone or line-in from the sound card, a
  monitor source, or the phone streaming into the bridge.

Bluetooth outputs are not repeated here. They belong to their device card;
listing them twice would make the page lie the moment a sink is renamed.

A card's **profile** and an output's **port** can be switched from their detail
panel. Both ask first, because the change is audible: the confirmation names the
outputs that may go silent. A Bluetooth card's profile is not offered — the
connection decides it, and the panel says so.

## The phone source

When the Sendspin source is enabled, a strip at the top shows whether a phone is
connected, what it is sending, and which players are armed to play it. It has
four states — off, waiting for a phone, streaming, and error — and an error names
the cause (for example a port already in use) with a next step.

Pairing a phone opens a two-minute window; the strip counts it down and can stop
it early. When the source is off entirely, the strip says where it is enabled
(Settings → Music Assistant) instead of showing controls that cannot work.

## When a section is empty

Empty and unavailable are different things, and the page never shows one as the
other:

- **No devices** means the bridge looked and found none.
- **A banner with a reason** means the bridge could not look: no D-Bus, no audio
  server, or the phone capture needs PipeWire nodes. The banner names the reason
  and the next step.
- **A “this bridge does not report audio” note** appears when an older bridge
  serves the page and the audio section is simply absent.

If a read fails while an earlier one succeeded, the page keeps the last data and
marks it as possibly stale rather than blanking the screen.

## What the page does not do

- It does not create, remove or rename players. That is the speakers dashboard.
- It does not change how a player routes its audio.
