The **Hardware** page shows the physical layer the bridge drives, whether or not
a player uses it: Bluetooth controllers, paired devices, and the audio devices of
the host with their inputs and outputs.

It is deliberately separate from the speakers dashboard. A speaker is a player;
a controller, a paired phone or a sound card is hardware. A device that no
player points at — a phone that is not streaming, a second adapter with nothing
on it — is still listed here.

## Controllers

One card per Bluetooth adapter:

- power state, and the power control;
- the friendly name you gave it in the configuration;
- the class of device the kernel reports (empty when the container has no
  `CAP_NET_RAW`);
- the paired devices that use this adapter;
- the Home Assistant area, when area mapping is configured.

A device can be bonded to more than one adapter; it then appears under each of
them, and its own card lists every adapter that holds the bond.

## Bluetooth devices

Paired and known devices, with what the bridge knows about each:

- connected, paired, bonded, trusted;
- battery level, when the device reports it over `org.bluez.Battery1`;
- signal strength, when the kernel reports it (a delta inside the BR/EDR golden
  receive range, not dBm);
- the codec and delay of the active transport;
- the **role** of the link — *Plays audio* when the bridge sends sound to it,
  *Receives audio* when it sends sound to the bridge. A device can show both.
- the player it backs, with a link to that player's card.

Actions: connect, disconnect, and forget the bond. Forgetting a bond that a
player uses asks first and names that player; the bridge does not silently leave
a player without a device.

## Audio

The audio server's own view of the host:

- **Cards** with their profiles and ports. When an output is silent because its
  active port is not available or its profile is `off`, this is where you see it.
- **Outputs** with state, volume, mute, and the player that uses each one.
- **Inputs** with their kind: a microphone or line-in from the sound card, a
  monitor source, or the phone streaming into the bridge.

Bluetooth outputs are not repeated here. They belong to their device card, and
listing them twice would make the page lie the moment a sink is renamed.

## Source

When the Sendspin source is enabled, a strip at the top shows whether a phone is
connected, what it is sending, and which players are armed to play it. Pairing a
phone and starting or stopping the source are available here.

## What the page does not do

- It does not create, remove or rename players. That is the speakers dashboard.
- It does not switch card profiles or ports yet; it explains the state instead.
- On a host without a readable Bluetooth stack or audio server it shows the
  reason instead of an empty list.
